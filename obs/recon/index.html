Aircraft Recon

Hurricane Hunter reconnaissance, straight from the data. The Current Mission tab leads with the National Hurricane Center Plan of the Day (which aircraft are tasked, where, and when) and spotlights the active or most recent pass, with a Last 10 min and a Full Mission view. The Storms tab plots any mission: a flight track drawn as standard wind barbs colored by flight-level wind on a tropical-cyclone scale, VDM center fixes labeled with sea-level pressure, dropsondes, and a stacked multi-panel time series (sea-level pressure and wind, SFMR and rain, temperature and dewpoint, static pressure and altitude). A Wind toggle switches the barbs and color between flight-level and SFMR surface wind. Every figure is one canvas, so you can right-click to save or use the Download PNG button to share exactly what you see.

Flight-level → surface wind

Estimate the surface wind from an aircraft flight-level wind using the mean adjustment factors of Franklin, Black & Valde (2003). Choose the flight level and storm region; surface wind ≈ flight-level wind × factor. These are climatological means — an individual reduction varies with the boundary layer, so treat the result as a first-order estimate, not a measurement.

Factors: Franklin, J. L., M. L. Black, and K. Valde (2003), “GPS Dropwindsonde Wind Profiles in Hurricanes and Their Operational Implications,” Weather and Forecasting, 18, 32–44 (Table 2).

About aircraft recon

NOAA and U.S. Air Force Reserve Hurricane Hunter aircraft fly directly into tropical cyclones to measure what satellites cannot. Each pass returns high-density observations (HDOBs) of flight-level and SFMR-estimated surface winds, vortex data messages (VDMs) fixing the storm center and its minimum sea-level pressure, and dropsondes profiling the atmosphere on the way down. Tasking is set the day before in the NHC / CARCAH Tropical Cyclone Plan of the Day. These figures are rendered in your browser from the parsed observations. SFMR surface winds are unreliable in heavy rain and at very high wind speeds; all obs are point-in-time aircraft measurements.
