ASCAT Ocean Winds

Scatterometer ocean-surface winds from the ASCAT instruments on EUMETSAT's MetOp-B and MetOp-C satellites, straight from the data, drawn as standard wind barbs colored by speed on a tropical-cyclone scale. Pick a basin for the most recent ASCAT-B + ASCAT-C coverage stitched together, or pick an active storm to center on it and see only its passes — then toggle a GOES / Himawari satellite backdrop, drag a box to zoom, hover any barb for the exact speed and direction, and use Download PNG to share what you see. ASCAT is a C-band scatterometer: it resolves the broad gale-force wind field well but underestimates the extreme winds in a tropical cyclone's core — a wind-field tool, not a peak-intensity tool. Rain- and quality-flagged cells are removed; swaths are intermittent and this feed runs about a day behind real time.

About ASCAT ocean winds

The Advanced Scatterometer (ASCAT) is a real-aperture radar that measures the roughness of the ocean surface, from which the 10-metre wind speed and direction are retrieved. These figures use the 12.5 km coastal Level-2 product from the EUMETSAT Ocean and Sea Ice Satellite Application Facility (OSI SAF), processed at KNMI and distributed through the KNMI Data Platform. Because ASCAT operates at C-band, it is relatively insensitive to rain — an advantage over Ku-band scatterometers — but the backscatter-to-wind relationship loses sensitivity above roughly 25 m/s, so ASCAT systematically underestimates the peak winds in an intense tropical cyclone's eyewall. Use it to map the extent of the gale- and storm-force wind field, not to estimate peak intensity. Wind directions are shown in the meteorological convention (barbs point toward the direction the wind is coming from). The optional satellite backdrop is the nearest GOES / Himawari storm-floater frame to each pass — its sensor, band and time are shown on the figure; barbs are the precise layer. Rain- and quality-flagged cells are removed. © EUMETSAT.
