What am I looking at?

Accumulated Cyclone Energy (ACE) is a measure of the total energy released by tropical cyclones during a season. It's the sum of the square of a storm's sustained winds, taken every six hours while the storm is at tropical-storm strength or stronger.

The blue bands show the statistical envelope of the last 30 years: the outer band is every season's min-to-max range, the middle band is the 10th–90th percentiles, and the inner band is the interquartile range (25th–75th percentile). The dashed blue line is the 30-year climatological mean.

The solid red line is the current season. The green line is the prior season for reference. The ranking table on the right shows where the current year stands versus every year back to 1945 at this same point in the calendar.

Where does the data come from?

Historical and provisional track data are from NOAA NCEI's IBTrACS v04r01 dataset. During an active season, the current-season line is kept fresh from live JTWC best-track files as soon as they're issued.
