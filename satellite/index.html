More observations

Observed passive-microwave TC overpasses (89 GHz PCT & 37 GHz color) and ASCAT scatterometer ocean-surface winds now have their own pages under Satellite — use the sub-navigation at the top.

Passive microwave (observed)

The Passive Microwave panel above is observed imagery of tropical cyclones. Active storms come from NASA GPM / PPS near-real-time (~1-3 h after each overpass); older storms come from the inter-calibrated NOAA/CIRA TC-PRIMED archive. Microwave sees through the high cloud that masks a storm's structure in infrared, so a single overpass reveals the eye, eyewall, and rainband organization directly. The 89 GHz PCT product is the polarization-corrected ice-scattering view (cold = deep convection, in the green-yellow-red-white scale over navy ocean); the 37 GHz Color product is the NRL two-channel composite (cyan-green ocean, magenta-pink heavy convection, white cold ice) that best shows the low-level circulation center. Polar-orbiting imagers (GPM GMI, GCOM-W AMSR2, the DMSP SSMIS series) catch a storm only once or twice a day, so each image is one satellite overpass, storm-centered, rendered from the gridded swath. The live tier pulls each pass straight from PPS near-real-time; the TC-PRIMED archive (research-tiered, post-season) backfills older storms. An ASCAT scatterometer view of near-surface wind is planned for this section.

Latest polar pass (250 m photographic)

The Latest Polar Pass panel above is a daily, high-resolution photographic snapshot per region: NASA's MODIS (Terra/Aqua, ~250 m) and VIIRS (SNPP/NOAA-20, ~375 m) true-color corrected-reflectance composites, served via NASA GIBS. Unlike the geostationary tool above, polar-orbiting sensors image each spot once or twice a day, so this is the freshest complete daily pass; gaps between orbit swaths are filled across sensors. Pick a region from the dropdown; the caption shows which sensor and day you're seeing.

How it works

Pick a bbox by dragging a rectangle on the map (the rectangle tool is the topmost button on the left toolbar). Choose a channel, True Color (RGB) for a natural photographic look (synthetic green on GOES, native green on Himawari; fades to clean-IR at night), IR Channel 13 for nighttime convection, upper-level water vapor for steering, or visible for daytime detail, and an enhancement preset (ignored for true color). Hit Render and the backend crops a tile just for your bbox and returns it as a PNG.

Any bbox size works: the backend picks the smallest GOES product family that covers your box (a Mesoscale sector if it fits in a ~12°×12° window, then CONUS, then Full Disk) so the source resolution is as fine as possible, and auto-downsamples oversized requests to stay within a 16M-pixel render budget. When that kicks in you'll see an "auto-downsampled" note on the result. Identical re-requests come back from cache instantly. Rate limit: 10 renders / minute / IP.

Coverage

Three satellite families today: GOES-East (currently GOES-19, with the GOES-16 archive back to 2017) covers ~135°W to 5°W, the Atlantic basin, eastern Pacific, and the Americas. GOES-West (currently GOES-18, with the GOES-17 archive back to 2019-02-12) covers ~160°E to ~-65°W via the antimeridian, Hawaii, Alaska, central + eastern Pacific. Himawari-Pacific (Himawari-9 since 2022-12-13, with the Himawari-8 archive back to 2017) covers ~60°E to 220°E, Western Pacific, East Asia, Oceania. The picker auto-routes by bbox center, so any zoom in any basin works without picking a satellite by hand. The Live Storm Floater and Mesoscale Sectors viewers below auto-track active systems and rapid-scan fixed sectors with built-in playback, scrubbing, and one-click GIF export. METEOSAT (Atlantic east / Africa / Europe) is next on the roadmap, along with full-disk presets and named-region floaters.
