    What am I looking at?

    This page packs two visualizations of Northeast Pacific tropical cyclone climatology.

    The live interactive chart at the top shows cumulative ACE versus the 1991–2020 statistical envelope (min–max, 10–90% and 25–75% bands plus the 30-year mean). The amber line is the current season; the violet line is last year for reference. Click any year in the in-chart ranking table to overlay that season's trajectory in hot pink — it's the fastest way to compare seasons head-to-head.

    The season profile below it is a static 4-panel snapshot of any one year:

    1. Cumulative ACE curve overlaid on the 1991–2020 climatology — nested blue bands for the min–max envelope, the 10th–90th percentile range, and the 25th–75th interquartile range, with dashed cyan and violet lines for the climo median and mean.
    2. Rank trajectory through the season: where this year sat versus every other in Northeast Pacific history at each calendar day (rank 1 = best at the top).
    3. Daily ACE intensity bars, with the season's most active day highlighted in amber.
    4. Storm Gantt timeline in a Wikipedia-style row-packed layout: every named system, color-coded by its peak Saffir–Simpson category, with a category legend strip below.

    Pick any past season from the rankings list on the right of the season profile (sorted by full-season ACE, back to 1876).

    Where does the data come from?

    Historical and provisional track data come from NOAA NCEI's IBTrACS v04r01 dataset. During an active season, the current-season line is kept fresh from live NHC ATCF best-track files as soon as they're issued.
