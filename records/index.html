How these records are computed

Atlantic and East Pacific records are built from NHC HURDAT2 through the last complete season, with IBTrACS v04r01 and live ATCF b-decks filling in the running season. West Pacific records come from the JTWC columns of IBTrACS from 1945 onward. Counts, ACE, PDI, duration, and concurrency use only the standard 00, 06, 12, and 18Z synoptic fixes, so every era is measured the same way; intensity extremes also consider special best-track entries such as landfall and peak fixes.

Winds in the Atlantic and East Pacific are NHC 1-minute sustained values. West Pacific boards prefer JTWC 1-minute winds and fall back to JMA or WMO 10-minute winds converted to a 1-minute equivalent by dividing by 0.88. The two averaging conventions are never mixed silently: each board's values follow one convention, noted under the board itself. ACE and PDI apply one subtropical-inclusion policy across all years, so some seasonal totals read slightly above the official operational numbers (Atlantic 2005: 250.1 here against 245.5 official). That is by design: internal consistency across eras matters more here than matching each year's operational bookkeeping.

A storm is credited to the basin and season of its genesis, so a Central Pacific crosser like Ioke 2006 counts in the East Pacific tables for its full lifetime. Records reach back to the start of each basin's best track, but seasons before continuous satellite coverage undercount real activity, especially storms that stayed at sea. Boards that would be distorted by that gap, such as fewest named storms or latest season start, are restricted to the satellite era.

Data: NHC HURDAT2 · NOAA NCEI IBTrACS v04r01 · live ATCF b-decks. Built for weather nerds by a weather nerd.
