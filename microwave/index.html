Passive Microwave

Observed passive-microwave imagery of tropical cyclones from NOAA/CIRA TC-PRIMED. Microwave sees through the high cloud that masks a storm's structure in infrared, so a single overpass reveals the eye, eyewall, and rainband organization directly. The 89 GHz PCT product is the polarization-corrected ice-scattering view (cold = deep convection, in the recognizable green-yellow-red-white scale over navy ocean); the 37 GHz Color product is the NRL two-channel composite (cyan-green ocean, magenta-pink heavy convection, white cold ice) that best shows the low-level circulation center. About passive microwave

Polar-orbiting microwave imagers (GPM GMI, GCOM-W AMSR2, and the DMSP SSMIS series) measure brightness temperature at frequencies where ice scattering in deep convection depresses the signal. The 89 GHz polarization-corrected temperature (PCT) combines the vertical and horizontal channels to remove the ocean's surface-emissivity signal, so what remains is the convective ice-scattering structure: the colder the PCT, the deeper the convection. The 37 GHz color composite blends the 37 GHz vertical and horizontal channels into an RGB that makes the warm low-level rain field and the storm's circulation center stand out. Each image here is one satellite overpass, storm-centered, rendered from the gridded swath; coverage is whatever the satellite caught on that pass. Imagery is provided by NOAA/CIRA TC-PRIMED for research and educational purposes.
